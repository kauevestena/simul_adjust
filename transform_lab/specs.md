- a new simulator its called "transform_lab". Home shall be at transform_lab/index.html
-  The idea is to transform between 2 sets of the same points in different coordinate frames. So there's the need for 3 rotations and 3 translations. Optionally a global scale factor can be included, enabling for as an example transforming directly from centimeters to meters. (one can choose the units of both of the input sets, and the units of the output set, and the scale factor will be automatically calculated, or they will be pretransformed and the scale factor removed).
- This is meant for students of "Ajustmento de Observações 2" discipline.   
- The "combinado" and "parametrico" solutions shall be compared, here some difference is expected as one set (the reference one needs to be declared as constants in "parametrico", thus fixed, but in "combinado" both can have their stochastic model - the variance-covariance matrix - declared).
- In the example, the reference set is generated in an a4 sheet as described in transform_lab/foundation/target_grid/coordinate_grid_report.md you may search the web and discover how precise are laser-printed distances in a4 sheets. They were printed in adhesive paper to a rigid and very flat surface, so they're expected to be very,very precise, maybe an educated guess is that the precision is in the order of 0.1 mm, but this is just a guess, and it may be even better than that. The reference set was generated in centimeters.
- The second is meant to be collected with a reflectorless total station, and the coordinates are expected to be in meters. So there's a shared core with other simulators like "intersecao_re_3D" and "ajusta_planos", on many important stuff like transforming raw total station measurements to a local coordinate frame, and the stochastic model of the measurements, and the stochastic model of the transformed coordinates, and the stochastic model of the transformed coordinates in the local frame. The "transform_lab" simulator is meant to be a playground for students to understand how to transform between 2 sets of points in different coordinate frames, and how to compare the results of different methods.
- The reference set shall be preloaded in the simulator, some points might not be colleted in the second set, that can also include points with no correspondence, that shall only be transformed having the stochastic model propagated. Some reference points can be disabled for the use in the adjustment, therefore serving as check points, the user shall have a slider from 5 to 5% to ask to randomly choose matches to be used as check points.
- since the reference points are very precise, the distance between any pair of points is very well known (this length can  also have its uncertainty propagated from the stochastic model of the reference points). So a CSV containing all possible pair, their distances and the uncertainty of the distances shall be generated. Those distances are going to be used to introduce a concept for the students: the constraint equations. They're called "injunções" in the Gemael Book. So we are going to compare the different results, both using them and not using them. The students shall be able to choose the number of constraint equations to be used: all; just neighbouring points; just horizontal and vertical from neighbouring points; or a random percentage.
- different parametrizations for the rotation shall be included: euler angles, quaternions, rotation matrix and axis-angle. Euler shall be the default as there's no constraint equations to be declared.
- 2D and 3D views are needed, and also a histogram of the residuals.
- There's shall be a separated page with the theory (acessible from the topbar, near the "go back" button), with all the models in both "combinado" and "parametrico" methods, described both in matricial form and algebraic form.
- There shall be a comparison separated page  (acessible from the topbar, near the "go back" button), that will compare any two type of solutions, so probably a modal to select the configurations and presets are needed.
